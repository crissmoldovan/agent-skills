# Choosing perspectives

The test for a perspective set is not "are these real job titles". It is **would these
two disagree**. If not, one of them is redundant and you are paying twice for one read.

## What makes a perspective produce different output

Three things, in decreasing order of effect:

1. **What they are exposed to.** A compliance reviewer notices retention and deletion
   because they will be asked to attest to them. A support engineer notices what a
   customer will quote back at them. Exposure drives attention more than expertise does.
2. **Whether they are subject to the thing.** A reader who would be *recorded by* a
   system reads it differently from one who would *read* it. This perspective is the one
   most often missing, and it produces findings the others structurally cannot.
3. **Time horizon.** Someone answering for this in three years asks different questions
   from someone shipping on Friday.

Job title alone is the weakest of the four signals and the one people reach for first.

## A set that disagrees

For most artefacts, four to six perspectives is enough, chosen so their interests
conflict:

| Perspective | Positioned to see |
| --- | --- |
| **The inheritor** | Why is this like this, and what did we already rule out |
| **The dissenter** | Where this is used against the person it records |
| **The operator** | What happens at 2am when it misbehaves |
| **The attester** | What can actually be evidenced under scrutiny |
| **The outsider** | What is unintelligible to someone who was not here |

**The dissenter is not optional.** Without a perspective that stands to lose from the
artefact, a fan-out is a hype loop with citations — every reader wants it to work, so
every reader finds ways it could.

## Dials

Modifiers change **where a perspective looks, never what it treats as true.** That
distinction is what keeps them safe: aim divergence at the search space, not at the
evidence rules.

| Dial | Range | Changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Divergence** | conventional → out-of-the-box | How far from the obvious case they hunt |
| **Rigour** | practitioner → academic | Lived friction versus named general classes |
| **Charity** | adversarial → generous | Whether premises are granted or attacked |
| **Horizon** | immediate / seasonal / multi-year | Today's friction versus the long-lived artefact |
| **Disposition** | burned / neutral / invested | What feels salient right now |

**Honest status: unvalidated.** The one measurement of these held a perspective constant
and varied four dials at once, at n=3. It cannot attribute any effect to any single dial,
and it credits the perspective with nothing since the perspective never varied. Treat the
dials as advisory vocabulary for shaping a prompt, not as a mechanism with demonstrated
effects.

Two tensions follow from the contract rather than from measurement. Both are
**predictions** — worth acting on because the countermeasures cost nothing, and worth
labelling because neither has been tested:

- **Divergence should fight specificity.** The genuine edges of a search space are the
  cases with no artefact to name — so a high-divergence reader is steered toward material
  it must then label `constructed`, which gets discounted. The stable strategy becomes
  mid-divergence content with divergent adjectives: the appearance of exploration without
  the substance. This is an argument from the incentives the contract creates, not an
  observed outcome.
- **Incoherent combinations should produce noise.** An attester at maximum charity, or a
  marketer at minimum, is a reader who cannot hold their own position. Refuse the
  combination rather than obeying it — cheap insurance against an untested failure, not a
  documented one.

## Fan-out discipline

If you run several:

- **Do not cross-product.** Perspectives times settings times dials explodes. Assign one
  combination per reader and spread them so no dial level is confounded with a
  perspective.
- **Keep them blind to each other.** Independence is what makes agreement mean anything.
- **Vary something.** Duplicates give several draws from one distribution at full price.
- **Build in a control.** Hold one variable constant across two readers. If their output
  is near-identical, the variable is doing nothing, and you learn that for the cost of
  nothing extra.
- **Stage the first batch.** Run one, check the grounding is real, then commit to the
  rest. One round trip against a whole wasted fan-out.

## Reading the results

Not by counting votes.

| Pattern | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| Agreement across **opposed interests** | The strongest signal available — but check whether the agreed frame came from the briefing first |
| A finding from **one perspective only** | Often the sharpest. The one reader positioned to see it was the only one who could. Do not discount by count |
| **Anti-scenarios clustering** | Where the artefact is genuinely weak |
| A perspective reporting **"this adds nothing for me"** | A scope signal, and the one result shape a briefing alone cannot produce |
| A **checkable structural claim** | Verify it directly. This is where the value concentrates |

**Verify before relaying.** A fan-out produces leads. Claims that a section contradicts
another, that a term appears nowhere, that a function cannot do what it promises — those
are checkable in seconds, and checking them is the difference between a review and a pile
of opinions.
